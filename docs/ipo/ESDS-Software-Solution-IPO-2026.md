# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**ESDS Software Solution** is a 21-year-old, Nashik-headquartered operator of five Tier-3 data
centres (~7 MW) selling cloud infrastructure (IaaS), managed services and SaaS, principally to Indian
enterprises, government bodies and BFSI institutions. It is raising **₹720 crore entirely as fresh
capital — there is no OFS whatsoever**, and roughly ₹576 crore of it goes into cloud and data-centre
equipment to roughly double capacity to 14–20 MW.

**The investment case rests on one question: is the last three years of margin expansion repeatable,
or was it a one-off harvest of old, largely-depreciated assets?**

The numbers are startling. EBITDA margin went **12.3% (FY23) → 26.6% (FY24) → 42.9% (FY25) → 49.6%
(FY26)** [C from R], and PAT went from a **₹22.5 crore loss in FY23 to ₹120.82 crore in FY26**.
Revenue grew at a much more ordinary **30.6% CAGR** over the same period [C]. Margin expansion, not
volume, did almost all of the work.

Working back from the reported ROCE of 32.78%, I derive **depreciation & amortisation of roughly
₹55–65 crore, or ~12–14% of revenue** [C — derived, moderate confidence]. That is remarkably low for
a company running five data centres, and it points to the most plausible explanation for the margin
ramp: **ESDS has been filling data-centre capacity that was built years ago and is now largely written
down.** Rising utilisation against a near-fixed, already-depreciated cost base produces exactly this
profit curve.

If that reading is right, it has a direct and under-appreciated consequence. The ₹576 crore of new
equipment, on a typical 5–6 year server life, adds roughly **₹96–115 crore per year of fresh
depreciation** [C] — about *twice* the current D&A charge, and an amount comparable to the whole of
FY26 PAT. The new capacity is accretive only if it fills. **Utilisation of the new build is the
central risk, and it is the thing you cannot verify from outside.**

**Valuation.** At ₹429, the post-issue market capitalisation is **₹5,028 crore** [R], implying **41.6×
FY26 earnings, ~21.9× EV/EBITDA and ~10.6× sales** [C]. Against the only listed peer, **E2E Networks**
(P/E 404× trailing, ~72× on annualised Q1FY27, EV/EBITDA 53.6×, P/B 7.5×) [R, 27 Aug 2026], ESDS is
conspicuously cheaper. But E2E is an AI/GPU-cloud stock priced for perfection; being cheaper than it
is a weak test. In absolute terms 41.6× is a full price, and the IPO itself **dilutes ROE from a
reported 25.1% to roughly 9.7%** [C] because the raise nearly triples book value overnight.

**Scale is the structural problem.** At ~7 MW, ESDS holds well under 1% of Indian data-centre capacity.
Its competitive set — CtrlS, Nxtra (Airtel), Yotta, Sify, Web Werks — deploys in hundreds of megawatts,
backed by balance sheets ESDS cannot match. ₹720 crore buys ESDS 7–13 MW; a single hyperscale campus
is 100 MW+. ESDS's defence is niche: sovereign/MeitY-style government and BFSI workloads where
compliance, hand-holding and a patented vertically auto-scaling platform (SWARAJ, formerly eNlight)
matter more than raw scale. That niche is real, but it is a niche.

**Earnings-quality flags** (none disqualifying, all worth pricing): receivable days of **79 vs 25 for
E2E** [R]; **₹63.39 crore of unbilled revenue**, 13.4% of FY26 revenue [R/C]; top-10 customer
concentration of **45.4%** with the single largest at **15.9%** [R]; **96.72% of current assets
hypothecated** to lenders [R]; and a former-employee claim to **1% of equity or ₹18.48 crore** [R].
Reassuringly, my derived effective tax rate of **~30–32%** [C] argues *against* the PAT surge being a
deferred-tax artefact.

**Sentiment is internally contradictory and I would discount it heavily.** Reported GMP is **₹330,
implying a +77% listing pop** [R, 28 Aug 2026] — yet the book was only **~0.40× on Day 1 with QIB
(ex-anchor) at nil** [R]. A ₹720 crore issue genuinely headed for +77% does not open like that. The
grey market for a mid-size issue is thin and easily moved. **The anchor book is the more credible
signal**: ₹216 crore fully placed with 19 investors, **81.94% to domestic mutual funds** across 6
fund houses [R].

# 2. Understanding the Business

## What ESDS actually does

Strip away the AI vocabulary and ESDS is a **landlord and operator of computing capacity**. It owns and
runs five data centres, fills them with servers, and rents that capacity — plus the labour to manage it
— to organisations that would rather not run their own infrastructure.

Three revenue layers, ascending in stickiness and margin:

1. **Colocation / IaaS (infrastructure-as-a-service).** The base layer: rack space, power, cooling,
   bandwidth, and virtual machines on ESDS's own cloud platform. Priced per unit of compute/storage,
   billed monthly. This is where the capital goes and where the operating leverage lives.
2. **Managed services.** ESDS staff run the customer's environment — patching, backup, security
   operations, migration, compliance reporting. Labour-led, higher-touch, and the principal reason
   customers stay: it embeds ESDS in the customer's operating routine.
3. **SaaS and software.** Proprietary applications sold on subscription. Smallest layer, but it
   deepens lock-in and carries software-like gross margins.

## How the money is actually made

The economics are **fixed-cost-and-fill**. A data centre's cost is set the day it is commissioned:
building, power infrastructure, cooling, and the servers inside it. Those costs barely move with
utilisation. Revenue, however, scales directly with how much of that capacity is contracted.

This is why the margin curve looks the way it does. Every incremental rack sold into *existing*
capacity drops almost entirely through to EBITDA. Going from, say, 55% utilisation to 80% can take
EBITDA margin from the twenties to the high forties without any heroics — which is precisely the
FY24→FY26 pattern. **The same mechanism runs in reverse.** A new, empty 7 MW carries full cost and
zero revenue from day one.

**Therefore: capacity utilisation is the master variable of this business.** Not revenue growth, not
customer count — utilisation. It is also, unhelpfully, the number ESDS is least obliged to disclose.

## Customers — who pays, and what that implies

| Segment | Share of FY26 revenue | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | **55.09%** [R] | Mid-market Indian businesses; competitive, price-sensitive, moderate switching costs |
| Government | **27.37%** [R] | Long tenders, slow payment, but very sticky and compliance-gated |
| BFSI | **17.53%** [R] | RBI-regulated workloads; highest switching costs, highest audit burden |

**Government + BFSI = 44.9% of revenue** [C]. This is the strategic core. Both segments require data
residency in India, empanelment/compliance credentials, and auditability — requirements that
*exclude* offshore hyperscalers from a meaningful slice of workloads and that a US-headquartered
provider cannot easily satisfy for a state government department. **This is ESDS's real moat, and it
is regulatory rather than technological.**

It is also the source of the receivables problem. Government entities pay on government timelines.
79-day DSO is not incompetence; it is the price of the segment.

- Client base: **1,300+** enterprise, government and BFSI clients [R]
- Top client: **15.93%** of FY26 revenue [R]
- Top 10 clients: **45.36%** (FY26), **49.34%** (FY25), **37.38%** (FY24) [R, year-order corrected by
  me via check #8]

Concentration **worsened sharply in FY25 then eased slightly in FY26**. It remains high: losing the
single largest account removes roughly one rupee in six of revenue.

## Technology and IP

The platform is **SWARAJ Cloud** (formerly **eNlight**), a *vertically auto-scaling* cloud —
**patented in both India and the United States** [R]. Vertical auto-scaling means a running VM can be
given more CPU/RAM on the fly without a reboot, rather than the horizontal "add another instance"
model the hyperscalers use.

The commercial significance is specific: it lets ESDS bill customers for *consumption* rather than
*provisioned capacity*, which is genuinely attractive to a government department with spiky, seasonal
load and a fixed annual budget. **A granted US patent is a real, verifiable asset** — but it is a
feature, not a platform. It does not stop AWS, Azure or CtrlS from competing on price and scale.

## What is driving it

- **Data localisation.** The DPDP Act framework and RBI's data-residency requirements legally compel
  certain data to remain in India. This is a *legislated* demand floor, and the most durable driver.
- **Hyperscale capital.** Microsoft and Google have committed **US$30 bn+** to Indian infrastructure [R].
- **Connectivity.** ~**220 Tbps** of new subsea capacity landing at Chennai and Mumbai [R].
- **AI workloads.** GPU demand is reshaping power and cooling requirements — an opportunity for those
  who can fund it, an obsolescence threat for those who cannot.
- **Enterprise cloud migration**, still early by developed-market standards.

## Classification

**Secular growth — but with a consolidating, capital-intensive structure.**

Secular because demand is driven by legislation and structural digitisation rather than the business
cycle. But the industry is simultaneously **consolidating around balance-sheet size**. Entry barriers
are capital and power access, not technology. Returns accrue to whoever can fund the next 100 MW.

**The uncomfortable implication for ESDS:** it is a genuinely secular market, and ESDS is genuinely
sub-scale within it. Sector tailwind and company positioning point in different directions, and an
investor must hold both thoughts simultaneously.

## The competitive set

| Category | Players |

| **Listed peer (RHP-designated)** | **E2E Networks Ltd** — the only listed peer ESDS itself names [R] |
| Large unlisted DC operators | **CtrlS, Nxtra by Airtel, Yotta, Web Werks, CyFuture** [R] |

**Why E2E is a fair comparator — and where it breaks down.** E2E is Indian, listed, cloud-
infrastructure, similar revenue scale (~₹627 cr annualised vs ESDS ₹472 cr), and competes for
overlapping customers. That makes it the right *starting* point. But E2E has pivoted hard into **GPU/AI
compute**, which carries higher margins (75% EBITDA in Q1FY27) and a very different multiple.
**Comparing ESDS's 41.6× to E2E's 404× and concluding "cheap" would be exactly the analytical error the
brief warns against.** E2E is priced on an AI narrative; ESDS is not. The comparison sets an upper
bound on sentiment, not a fair value.

**Why the hyperscalers are indirect, not direct.** AWS/Azure win on scale, breadth and price for
general enterprise workloads and ESDS cannot compete there. They are structurally handicapped only in
sovereign/regulated workloads. This bifurcation defines where ESDS can and cannot win.

## The depreciation derivation — and why it matters most

Working backwards from reported ratios [C, moderate confidence]:

```
Reported ROCE                = 32.78%
Average capital employed     ≈ avg equity ₹481 cr + avg debt ₹52.8 cr  = ₹534 cr
⇒ EBIT                       ≈ 0.3278 × 534                            = ₹175 cr
⇒ D&A                        = EBITDA ₹234.23 − EBIT ₹175              ≈ ₹59 cr
⇒ D&A as % of revenue        = 59 ÷ 472.2                              ≈ 12.5%
```

*(Average equity derived from ROE: ₹120.82 ÷ 0.2512 = ₹481 cr. Closing equity from D/E:
₹42.92 ÷ 0.08 = ₹536 cr. Average debt from reported borrowings ₹62.71 cr (FY25) and ₹42.92 cr (FY26).)*

**₹59 crore of D&A on five Tier-3 data centres is low — strikingly so.** Comparable colocation
operators typically run D&A at 20–30% of revenue. Three explanations are possible:

1. **Legacy assets are largely written down.** ESDS has operated since 2005; the Nashik facility is
   old. Assets past their depreciable life carry revenue but no charge. **This is the most likely
   explanation, and it fits the margin curve perfectly.**
2. **Leased rather than owned capacity**, shifting cost from D&A into operating expense.
3. **Long assumed useful lives**, which would be an accounting-aggressiveness flag.

I cannot distinguish between these without the RHP's fixed-asset schedule. **[NA] — and it is the single
most valuable thing an investor could check before applying.**

**The forward consequence is the same under explanations (1) and (3):**

```
New equipment from IPO proceeds                     = ₹576 cr
Typical server/cloud-hardware life                  = 5–6 years
⇒ Incremental annual D&A once fully deployed        ≈ ₹96–115 cr  [C]
Current D&A                                         ≈ ₹59 cr
⇒ Total D&A post-deployment                         ≈ ₹155–174 cr  (≈3× current)
```

That incremental **₹96–115 crore of annual depreciation is roughly 80–95% of the whole of FY26 PAT.**
Reported EPS will be flat or down for at least a year *unless* the new capacity fills fast. Sizing the
offset:

```
Capacity 7 MW → 14 MW (doubling)  ⇒ revenue capacity ≈ ₹472 cr → ~₹900 cr at full utilisation
Incremental revenue at maturity                      ≈ ₹430 cr
× incremental EBITDA margin ~50%                     ≈ ₹215 cr
− incremental D&A                                    ≈ ₹105 cr
⇒ Incremental EBIT at full utilisation               ≈ ₹110 cr  [C, illustrative]
```

**So the capex is strongly accretive at full utilisation and strongly dilutive at low utilisation.**
The entire equity story reduces to one number ESDS does not publish. That is why the long-term verdict
is Neutral rather than Positive: this is not a judgment about quality, it is an admission that the
decisive variable is unobservable from outside.

## The two findings that matter

**1. The tax rate exonerates the profit surge — this is the most reassuring finding in the report.**

The obvious suspicion about PAT rising 117% while EBITDA rose 51% is that the gap came from a
below-the-line accounting benefit — most plausibly deferred-tax-asset recognition against the FY23

```
EBITDA                                    ₹234.23 cr [R]
− D&A (derived)                           ~₹59 cr    [C]

= Tax                                     ~₹57 cr
⇒ Effective tax rate                      ~32%       [C]
```

**An effective rate above the statutory 25.17% is the opposite of a tax-flattered result.** The PAT
growth appears to be genuine operating performance amplified by falling interest cost. *(Caveat: this
chain rests on a derived D&A figure. If actual D&A is lower, the implied tax rate rises further —

is [NA]. If unbilled revenue is growing faster than revenue, recognition is running ahead of
collection. **Check the ageing schedule in the RHP.**

**Overall earnings quality: Moderate-to-Good, with two open questions.** Nothing here looks
manipulated. The deleveraging and the high tax rate both point toward real profits. But the working-
capital position is loose, and the absence of a cash-flow statement means I am inferring rather than
verifying.

# 25. What Could Go Right / What Could Go Wrong

## Bull case — what could go significantly right

1. **New capacity fills fast.** 7 MW → 14 MW absorbed within 18 months would add ~₹430 cr of revenue
   at ~50% incremental EBITDA margin — roughly **doubling EBITDA** [C].
2. **AI/GPU workloads land in India's sovereign segment.** Government AI mandates requiring
   India-resident compute would play directly to ESDS's regulatory positioning.
3. **Mix shifts toward managed services and SaaS**, lifting margins above the 49.6% base and
   deepening switching costs.
4. **Data-localisation tightens further**, widening the perimeter from which hyperscalers are excluded.
5. **Working capital normalises.** DSO from 79 → 50 days would release **~₹37 cr of cash** [C] and
   materially improve the quality score.
6. **Multiple re-rating toward the peer.** ESDS at even half of E2E's forward EV/EBITDA (~26.7×) would
   imply ~₹530/share [C].
7. **Government/BFSI wins de-risk concentration** while adding the stickiest revenue in the business.
8. **Post-IPO balance-sheet strength enables an acquisition** of a sub-scale regional operator,
   buying capacity below replacement cost.
9. **Index and institutional inclusion** post-listing broadens the shareholder base.

**Single biggest upside catalyst: the new 7 MW filling faster than the market expects.** It is the
only variable that simultaneously drives revenue, margin and multiple, and it is unobservable today —
which is precisely why it can surprise.

## Bear case — what could go significantly wrong

1. **New capacity sits empty.** ~₹105 cr of fresh annual depreciation [C] against underutilised
   assets would compress PAT toward ₹40–60 cr and collapse the multiple. **This is the primary risk.**
2. **Margin normalisation is misread as deterioration.** Even a *successful* build compresses FY27
   reported EPS as depreciation lands ahead of revenue. Markets punish first and understand later.
3. **A top-3 customer is lost.** With the largest client at 15.93%, a single departure removes ~₹75 cr
   of high-margin revenue [C].
4. **Working capital deteriorates further.** DSO past 90 days with unbilled revenue rising faster than
   revenue would signal recognition running ahead of collection.
5. **A well-funded competitor targets the government/BFSI niche.** Nxtra or CtrlS pricing aggressively
   into sovereign workloads would attack the only segment where ESDS has a moat.
6. **A cybersecurity incident.** For a provider hosting BFSI and government data, a serious breach is
   close to terminal for the compliance-led franchise.
7. **Multiple compression.** If E2E de-rates from 404×, the relative-value argument supporting ESDS
   evaporates overnight.
8. **Technology obsolescence strands the capex.** ₹576 cr of hardware overtaken by a GPU generation
   shift within its 5–6 year depreciable life.
9. **Further dilution.** The next capacity doubling needs more capital; another raise at a lower price
   would compound the damage.
10. **Lock-in expiry (~March 2027).** A substantial pre-IPO non-promoter base becomes free to sell.

**Single biggest downside risk: the new capacity fails to fill while its depreciation lands in full.**
Note the symmetry — **the biggest upside and the biggest downside are the same variable in opposite
directions.** That is the definition of a binary execution bet, and it is why position sizing matters
more than direction here.

# 26. Final Investment Verdict

## Overall: 🟡 **APPLY / BUY WITH CAUTION**

**A good business, honestly structured, at a full price — with the decisive operating variable
undisclosed and the cash-flow statement unavailable.**

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **For listing gains** | 🟡 **APPLY WITH CAUTION** — favourable structure (zero OFS, full MF-heavy anchor book, modest float, defensible relative valuation) supports a positive debut. **But the +77% GMP is not credible and must not drive sizing.** Apply for one lot; treat any premium as a windfall. |
| **For a 3–5 year investment** | 🟡 **NEUTRAL** — the quality is real but the price pays for success in advance. **Better approached on the secondary market after two or three quarters of disclosed results** — specifically CFO, capacity utilisation and the FY27 depreciation charge. |
| **At the lower band (₹408)** | 🟡 **APPLY / BUY WITH CAUTION** — ~39.8× FY26 P/E, roughly at my fair-value midpoint of ₹390. Marginally acceptable. |
| **At the upper band (₹429)** | 🟠 **APPLY FOR LISTING GAINS ONLY** — 41.6×, ~10% above my midpoint. Adequate for a flip; insufficient for a hold. |

## What would change this conclusion

**Upgrade to 🟢 BUY if:**
- The RHP shows **CFO/PAT above 1.0×** for FY25 and FY26 *(this single disclosure would move business
  quality from 6.6 to ~7.0 and remove my largest reservation)*
- **QIB subscription exceeds 20×**, confirming institutional conviction at ₹429
- Capacity utilisation is disclosed **above 80%**, indicating the new build addresses proven demand
- Contracted revenue or committed order book for the new capacity is disclosed
- The price is available **below ₹360** post-listing

**Downgrade to 🟠 AVOID AT IPO PRICE if:**
- **QIB comes in under 3×**, leaving the GMP institutionally unsupported
- The RHP reveals **CFO materially below PAT**, or unbilled revenue growing faster than revenue
- Depreciation policy shows **useful lives longer than industry norms** (which would mean current
  margins are an accounting choice, not an operating achievement)
- Related-party transactions or auditor observations emerge that my FY22-vintage evidence could not
  surface
- The ex-employee equity claim is shown to be materially provisioned or advanced

**The honest summary: I have high confidence in the income statement, moderate confidence in the
balance sheet, and no visibility on cash flow. That distribution of confidence is what produces a
Neutral long-term view on a business I otherwise like.**

# 30. The 10 Things to Know Before Applying

1. **It is a 100% fresh issue with zero OFS.** Every rupee goes to the company; the promoters sell
   nothing and dilute from 46.06% to ~39.5%. **In a month where a ₹1,757 cr peer issue was 91% OFS,
   this is a genuinely strong alignment signal.**

2. **Profit growth is a margin story, not a volume story — and margin stories end.** Revenue compounded
   at 30.6%; EBITDA compounded at 107.6%. EBITDA margin went 12.3% → 49.6% in three years. **It cannot
   go to 150%.** Anyone extrapolating +117% PAT growth is extrapolating an arithmetic impossibility.

3. **Part of that margin is a legacy-asset artefact that this very IPO will unwind.** Derived D&A is
   only ~12.5% of revenue — low for five data centres, and consistent with assets built years ago and
   largely written down. The ₹576 cr of new equipment adds **~₹96–115 cr of fresh annual depreciation
   — roughly 80–95% of all of FY26 PAT.**

4. **The whole investment reduces to one undisclosed number: capacity utilisation.** Doubling 7 MW to
   14 MW is strongly accretive if filled and strongly dilutive if not. **ESDS does not disclose it,
   and you cannot know it before applying.**

5. **ROE drops from 25.1% to about 9.7% the day the money lands.** The raise nearly triples book value.
   The headline return ratio in the marketing will not exist by the December 2026 quarter.

6. **The cash-flow statement was unavailable to me, and that is the biggest gap in this analysis.**
   Receivables run at **79 days versus 25 for the listed peer**, with **₹63.39 cr of unbilled
   revenue** (13.4% of revenue) — roughly **₹165 cr, or 1.4× FY26 PAT, tied up in working capital.**
   Debt falling 63% in two years is supportive indirect evidence, but it is not verification.

7. **The +77% GMP is not credible, and the Day-1 book quietly says so.** ₹330 premium implies listing
   at ~72× FY26 earnings. Retail sat at 0.54× on Day 1. **A genuinely expected 77% pop does not
   produce that book.** The grey market for a ₹720 cr issue is thin and easily moved.

8. **The anchor book is the signal actually worth weighting.** ₹216 cr fully placed with 19 investors
   at the top of the band, **81.94% to domestic mutual funds** across six fund houses — institutions
   that did real diligence and accepted a lock-in.

9. **ESDS is structurally sub-scale, and the IPO does not fix it.** At ~7 MW it holds well under 1% of
   Indian data-centre capacity while CtrlS, Nxtra and Yotta deploy in the hundreds of megawatts. Its
   defence is regulatory — sovereign and BFSI workloads offshore hyperscalers legally cannot take —
   covering 44.9% of revenue. **The other 55.09% has no moat.**

10. **There is no margin of safety at either end of the band.** Fair value ₹330–450, midpoint ~₹390,
    against a band of ₹408–429 — **−4.6% at the floor, −10.0% at the cap.** My base case returns
    **+0.9% per annum over three years.** The business is good; the price already assumes it.
