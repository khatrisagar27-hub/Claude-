# Discovery Call Script (20 Minutes)

**Goal:** Diagnose their situation, present your solution, close or set up the follow-up sequence.  
**Format:** Use this as a reference — internalize the structure, not the exact words.  
**Rule:** Ask more than you talk. They should speak 70% of the time.

---

## Pre-Call Setup

- [ ] Review their LinkedIn profile (2 min)
- [ ] Note one specific thing to reference early (post, career change, company milestone)
- [ ] Have your Calendly link ready in case they want to re-book
- [ ] Have your payment link ready (Stripe) if they close on the call
- [ ] Remove distractions — no notifications, no second screen during the call

---

## Call Structure

### 0–2 min: Frame the Call

*"Thanks for making the time, [Name]. I've got us blocked for 20 minutes.*

*My goal today is to understand your situation well enough to tell you honestly whether what I do is a fit. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you — I'd rather you spend 20 minutes and walk away with clarity than feel like you sat through a pitch.*

*Sound good?"*

Then: **"Tell me a bit about what's going on right now with [their problem area]."**

— And stop talking.

---

### 2–8 min: Diagnose (The Four Questions)

Ask these in order. Do not rush. Let silences breathe.

**Q1 — Current State:**
*"Walk me through what's happening right now with [their problem area]."*

Listen for: specific symptoms, timeline, emotional weight. Take notes.

**Q2 — What They've Tried:**
*"What have you already tried to solve this?"*

Listen for: their self-awareness level, what hasn't worked, why. This tells you how sophisticated they are and what NOT to recommend.

**Q3 — Cost of Inaction:**
*"What does this problem cost you — in revenue, time, or stress — if it's still unsolved 6 months from now?"*

**This is the most important question.** Make them quantify the pain. Their number becomes your pricing anchor.

Write down their exact answer. You'll repeat it back when you name your price.

**Q4 — Value of the Solution:**
*"What would solving this actually be worth to you — if everything went right?"*

Let them name the number. Never name a value first.

---

### 8–12 min: Present Your Approach

One calm paragraph. Do not oversell. Do not list every feature.

*"Here's what I typically see with [company type] in your situation. [1-sentence diagnosis — connecting what they told you to the root cause you know how to fix].*

*The way I address this in the Strategy Sprint is [specific approach — 2–3 sentences]. We do [X], then [Y], then [Z]. Most clients I work with come out with [specific outcome] by the end of the 30 days.*

*Does that map to what you're trying to solve?"*

— If yes, move forward. If no, ask what's missing and address it.

---

### 12–15 min: The Close

Wait for them to ask about price, or create a natural pause and say:

*"Before I share investment — you mentioned this is costing you [repeat their Q3 answer]. And solving it is worth [repeat their Q4 answer]. With that context —*

*The Strategy Sprint is $2,500. That includes [the 3 deliverables]. Given the math you just gave me, it's a straightforward decision.*

*Do you want to move forward?"*

**Then stop talking. Silence is not awkward — it's the close working. Do not break it.**

---

### 15–20 min: Handle Objections

**"I need to think about it"**
*"Totally fair. What specifically do you need to think through? I'd rather we address it now than have you sit with an unanswered question all week."*

[If they name something specific — answer it directly.]
[If they give a vague answer — the real objection is usually price or trust. Ask: "Is it a budget question or more about whether this will work for your situation?"]

---

**"It's too expensive"**
*"What were you expecting to invest?"*

[Let them name a number. Don't guess.]

- If they say something close ($1,500–2,000): *"Here's what I can do — I can offer a stripped-down entry version at $1,500 that covers the audit and roadmap only, no sessions. Would that work?"*
- If they say $500 or less: They were never the right client. Politely disqualify. *"That's not a fit for what I do — I'd be underdelivering at that budget. Happy to point you toward some resources that might help."*

---

**"I need to check with my co-founder / partner"**
*"Makes sense — this should be a joint decision. What does your gut say right now, just between us?"*

[This surfaces the real objection. If their gut is yes but they need sign-off, offer: "Can I send something to both of you? Even a quick 15-min call together might save you the back-and-forth."]

---

**"Can you send me more information?"**
This usually means: I'm not convinced yet, but I don't want to say no.

*"Of course — what specifically would help you decide? I want to make sure I send what's actually useful, not just a generic overview."*

[If they can't say what they need — the objection isn't information. Probe deeper.]

---

### If They Close on the Call

*"Perfect. I'll send you an invoice via Stripe right now — takes 2 minutes to pay. Once that's done, I'll send you an onboarding doc and we'll pick the dates for all three sessions. Sound good?"*

[Send Stripe payment link immediately while they're on the call or within 5 minutes after.]

---

### If They Don't Close on the Call

*"No pressure at all — take the time you need. I'll send you a summary of what we discussed and the next step. My follow-up email lands tonight."*

[Trigger Email Sequence A — see `email-sequences.md`]
