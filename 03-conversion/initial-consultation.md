# Initial Consultation — Structure and Script

**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Format:** In-person at your office or theirs, or video call  
**Fee:** None (this is professional courtesy — and it converts naturally when done right)  
**Goal:** Diagnose their tax situation, identify specific legitimate opportunities, position your Annual Engagement as the logical next step

---

## Before the Meeting

**Request from them (send after confirming the meeting):**

*"To make our discussion as useful as possible, please bring / share the following:*
- *Last 2 years' ITR acknowledgements (personal and business)*
- *Current salary/remuneration structure (Form 16 or breakdown)*
- *Last year's advance tax challans*
- *Basic P&L summary of the business"*

Most prospects won't bring everything. That's fine — whatever they share is enough to demonstrate your value.

---

## Meeting Structure

### Part 1 — Understand Their Current Position (15 min)

Do not open with "how can I help you." Open with context-setting:

*"Thank you for taking the time. I've set aside 45 minutes — my goal is to understand your situation well enough to give you a clear picture of where you stand from a tax planning perspective. If there are obvious opportunities, I'll tell you specifically. If everything is already optimized, I'll tell you that too."*

Then ask:

1. *"What's the current structure — are you drawing salary from the company, or is it a mix of salary and dividend?"*

2. *"What did you pay approximately in income tax last year — personal tax, not company tax?"*

3. *"Is there a CA currently handling your tax returns? Are they also doing any active planning, or mostly compliance?"*
   (This tells you: is there a competing relationship? What gap exists?)

4. *"Has anyone reviewed your remuneration structure from a tax efficiency standpoint in the last 3 years?"*

Listen more than you talk in this section.

---

### Part 2 — Diagnose and Demonstrate (20 min)

Based on what they shared, identify 2–4 specific opportunities. Be concrete — section numbers, rough quantum of benefit, why they're currently missing it.

**The structure for each insight:**

*"One thing I notice: [observation about their structure]. Under [Section X], [what is legitimate]. In your situation, that means approximately [rough benefit]. Most people in your position aren't using this because [reason]. This alone is worth reviewing."*

**Common findings for ₹50 crore turnover business directors:**

- **HRA / Rent-free accommodation:** If they own their home but don't structure their CTC to include HRA properly — significant deduction lost. Section 10(13A).

- **LTA / Leave Travel:** Often completely unused or not structured in CTC. Section 10(5).

- **Medical reimbursement / Health insurance:** Section 80D for self, family, parents — often under-utilized.

- **HUF creation:** If they have ancestral property or have received gifts, an HUF can be a tax-efficient structure. HUF income taxed separately, has its own basic exemption and deductions.

- **Director remuneration vs. dividend:** Tax implications differ significantly under the current regime. Many promoter-directors are not optimized for their personal tax.

- **Section 37 — Business expenditure:** Items the business pays for that are legitimately deductible but often claimed personally (vehicle usage, professional subscriptions, certain travel).

- **Advance tax timing:** Many business owners overpay in Q4 and miss optimizing timing across the year.

- **Capital gains planning:** If they've sold or are planning to sell business assets, property, or investments — timing and classification matter enormously.

Be specific. Show your knowledge. Do not hold back to "keep them curious" — the depth of what you share in the consultation is what builds trust.

---

### Part 3 — Next Step (10 min)

*"Based on what you've shared, there are clearly areas that warrant a thorough review — [summarize 2–3 you identified]. To be precise about the actual quantum and how to implement correctly, I'd need to review your last 2 years' returns and financials in detail.*

*The right first step is a Tax Planning Audit. I go through your documents systematically, identify every legitimate optimization opportunity, and give you a written report with specific recommendations and projected savings. That takes about 2–3 weeks and my fee for the audit is ₹[25,000–50,000].*

*Once you have the report, you decide whether to implement yourself or have me manage the planning ongoing as part of an annual engagement."*

Then stop. Let them respond.

---

## Responses to Common Reactions

**"My current CA handles all this."**  
*"That's fine — many of my clients have compliance CAs. The planning side is a different specialization. If everything is already optimized, the audit will confirm that and give you peace of mind. If there are gaps, the report will make them clear."*

**"What does the annual engagement cost?"**  
*"It depends on the complexity of your structure — typically ₹1.5 to 3 lakh per year for businesses at your scale. The audit is the first step and it gives me the full picture to quote accurately."*

**"How much can you save me?"**  
*"I don't make claims before reviewing your specifics — that wouldn't be professional. What I can tell you is that for businesses in your bracket, I've consistently found planning opportunities that hadn't been addressed. The audit will tell you exactly what's available for your situation."*

**"Let me think about it."**  
*"Of course. I'll send you a brief note summarizing what we discussed today — the areas I flagged and what the audit process looks like. Take your time and come back to me when it makes sense."*

(Send a professional email summary within 24 hours — see below.)
